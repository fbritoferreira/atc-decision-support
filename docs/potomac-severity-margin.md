# The flagship reconstruction produces a warning, not a critical

Status: found 2026-08-25 by auditing the corpus table's "highest-severity
alert produced" column against what the detectors actually emit. Two rows were
wrong. No geometry has been changed, because correcting it properly needs data
this project does not hold; the paper now reports the measured severity and the
reason.

## What the audit found

| scenario | table claimed | actually emitted |
|---|---|---|
| Potomac mid-air 2025 | proximity conflict, critical | proximity conflict, **warning** |
| USAir 1493 / SkyWest 5569, 1991 | runway conflict, critical | runway conflict, **warning** |

Six other incident rows check out, and American 11 correctly emits nothing.

## Why the Potomac case is a warning, to three decimal places

The critical tier requires three conditions: convergence with time to closest
point of approach inside 45 seconds, horizontal miss distance at that point
inside DMOD of 0.5 NM, and vertical separation already inside 200 ft. The
encoded pair, measured:

- vertical separation 150 ft, inside the 200 ft envelope
- closing speed 3.81 NM per minute, time to closest approach **6.9 seconds**,
  far inside the 45-second gate
- horizontal miss distance at closest approach **0.506 NM**, against a DMOD of
  0.500 NM

It fails on the third condition by six thousandths of a nautical mile, about 36
feet. Two conclusions follow and both matter.

The first is that a critical-or-warning verdict decided by 36 feet is decided
by encoding noise rather than by doctrine. The aircraft positions in this
reconstruction were placed by hand from the investigation's narrative
description, and their precision is nowhere near 36 feet. Any claim that this
scenario demonstrates the critical tier firing on the accident would be
resting on a coincidence of hand-placement.

The second is sharper and is the reason not to fix this by nudging a
coordinate. **The encoded tracks miss each other by half a nautical mile.** The
actual event was a collision, so the real miss distance was zero. The
reconstruction reproduces the proximity of the accident without reproducing its
collision geometry, and that was invisible while nothing measured the miss
distance. Nudging a position until the alert turns critical would be fitting
the scenario to the desired output, which is the opposite of what the corpus is
for.

## What would fix it, and why it is not done here

The NTSB's final report and its accompanying track data give measured positions
and tracks for both aircraft. Encoding those would produce a converging pair
with a miss distance near zero, the critical tier would fire on doctrine rather
than on luck, and the scenario would then reproduce what happened rather than
something near it. That is the correct fix and it is a data-sourcing task, not
an editing task: the numbers have to come from the report.

Until then the corpus table reports the warning, the paper states the margin,
and a test pins the measured severity so that a future change to the geometry
or to DMOD announces itself instead of quietly flipping the flagship case.

## The general point

The corpus table's severity column had never been checked against detector
output. It was written when the claims were made and not re-derived when the
convergence test replaced the distance box, which is the same drift that left
four passages describing a detector that does not compute tau. A column
asserting what a system produces is a measurement and belongs under the same
rule as every other number in this project: regenerated by a script, not
remembered.
